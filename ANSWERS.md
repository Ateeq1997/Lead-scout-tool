## 4. Business Understanding

### What is Caprae's Mission?

Caprae Capital's mission is to prove that private equity value creation
doesn't have to come mainly from financial engineering — it can come from
operational and technological improvement inside the businesses they acquire.
Their SaaS and MaaS models are built around a multi-year view of ownership,
where the real value is created in the years after a deal closes, not at the
moment of acquisition. That's a different mindset from traditional PE, which
is often judged on the deal itself rather than what happens afterward.

A core part of that mission is helping the companies they invest in adopt
practical AI tools to improve decision-making and operations, rather than
treating AI as a buzzword. The handbook itself reflects this — the challenge
isn't a generic coding test, it's a live example of the kind of tool a
portfolio company might actually need. That combination of long-term
operating discipline and genuine tech adoption is what I read as the core of
their mission.

### Why do you want to work at Caprae Capital?

I want to work somewhere my code has to hold up against a real business
outcome, not just pass a code review. Caprae's model — building tools like
SaaSquatch in-house rather than renting someone else's — is exactly the kind
of engineering problem I want to be closer to: software that directly
supports deal sourcing, lead generation, and post-acquisition operations,
where the impact is measurable.

My background is full-stack development (React, Next.js, TypeScript,
Node.js/MERN), and I've specifically worked on GIS-based features and early
AI/RAG components in production applications, alongside entry-level AWS
work. That maps directly onto the kind of tooling Caprae builds for its own
deal pipeline and its portfolio companies — data-heavy, decision-support
software rather than pure consumer apps. I'd rather build things that
compound in value the way Caprae's own SaaS/MaaS model is designed to.

I also value the directness in how the handbook is written — it's clear
about what's expected and what "good" looks like. That kind of clarity
usually means the team itself operates the same way, which is the
environment I do my best work in.

### How is Caprae Changing the ETA Space and Broader PE?

Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition has traditionally been slow and
relationship-driven — a single searcher spending months sourcing and vetting
one deal at a time. Caprae's tech-enabled approach (building its own
sourcing tools like SaaSquatch rather than depending on legacy databases or
manual outreach) turns that into something closer to a repeatable, scalable
pipeline. That's a meaningful shift for the ETA space specifically, where
most players are still operating at boutique scale.

For the broader PE industry, the shift is in where value gets created. A
lot of PE is still judged on financial structuring at the time of the deal.
Caprae's model treats the acquisition as the start of a multi-year
operating relationship, and uses data and AI-driven tooling to actively
improve the businesses it holds — not just monitor them. Combining
technology-first sourcing with hands-on, long-horizon operating support is
what differentiates them from both traditional search funds and traditional
PE shops.
